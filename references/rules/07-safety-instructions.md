# STE Section 7: Safety Instructions (Rules 7.1–7.3)

| Rule | Summary |
|------|---------|
| **7.1** | Put WARNING before steps where injury or death can occur. Format: WARNING symbol + text, then the step |
| **7.2** | Put CAUTION before steps where equipment damage can occur. Format: CAUTION symbol + text, then the step |
| **7.3** | Use standard safety keywords. WARNING for personal safety, CAUTION for equipment damage. Do not use both for the same hazard |

## Deep Dive

**Formatting:** In plain text, write the keyword in bold uppercase followed by a colon, then the condition. The WARNING/CAUTION comes BEFORE the step it protects, separated from it:

```
**WARNING:** Make sure that the switch is OFF before you open the cover.

- Open the cover.
```

**Rule 7.1 — WARNING (personal safety):** Use when the reader or others can be injured or killed.

- "**WARNING:** Do not open the cover when the pump is ON."
- "**WARNING:** Make sure that the switch is OFF before you open the cover."

**Rule 7.2 — CAUTION (equipment damage):** Use when the equipment can be damaged.

- "**CAUTION:** Do not operate the pump without fluid."

**Rule 7.3 — one keyword per hazard:** Pick WARNING or CAUTION, not both. If a hazard can hurt both people and equipment, use WARNING (people matter more).

**State the condition before the action:** "before you open the cover" precedes "open the cover" in the WARNING above. Negative instructions are allowed in safety text ("Do not...") — safety is the standard exception to Rule 9.3.

**Do not use safety keywords in body text:** "Warning: the pressure is low" is a NOTE, not a safety instruction. Reserve WARNING/CAUTION for actual hazard steps.
