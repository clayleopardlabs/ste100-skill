# STE Section 6: Descriptive Writing (Rules 6.1–6.6)

| Rule | Summary |
|------|---------|
| **6.1** | Use descriptive writing to explain facts, functions, and conditions — not to give instructions |
| **6.2** | Use key words and key phrases to make the text easy to scan |
| **6.3** | Keep descriptive sentences to maximum 25-27 words |
| **6.4** | Use paragraphs to group related information. One topic per paragraph |
| **6.5** | Start each paragraph with a topic sentence that introduces the subject |
| **6.6** | Maximum 6 sentences per paragraph. Keep paragraphs short |

## Deep Dive

**Rule 6.1 — description vs. procedure:** Descriptive text answers "what is it / how does it work". Procedural text answers "what do I do". Do not mix them: a manual that describes the system in a paragraph followed by a bulleted procedure is correct STE. A paragraph that sneaks instructions into the description is not.

**Rule 6.2 — key words first:** Put the important words early in the sentence and repeat them. "The pump supplies fluid to the engine" scans better than "Fluid is supplied by the pump to the engine".

**Rules 6.4-6.6 — paragraph shape:** one topic per paragraph, topic sentence first, max 6 sentences. If a paragraph exceeds 6 sentences, split it into two paragraphs and give the second one its own topic sentence.

**Voice:** active voice "as much as possible" — this section is the ONLY place passive voice is tolerated at all. Prefer active everywhere.
